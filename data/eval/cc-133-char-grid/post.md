## LinkedIn
> Three of the eight characters in my hackathon game ran backwards, and we only noticed in playtesting.
>
> The game is a ninja-warrior physics game where a real park, a real street or a photo of a bench becomes the course, built for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon. Players can also bring their own character: a picture becomes a 3D model, gets a skeleton, and runs the course.
>
> The clip shows the three that went wrong, a banana, a skater and a potato blob, in the real Course. The left column is how they came out of the maker: the skin faced one way and the skeleton pushed the other way, so the feet and legs looked broken. The middle and right columns are the same characters after the fix, running right and running left.
>
> The cause was small. The maker straightens a model by fitting the smallest box around it, and a box has no front, so it was right about half the time. The fix measures which end of the skin the shoes point to when the character loads, and turns it around if it is backwards.
>
> Every take is filmed on a stepped clock, one picture per game step, so the speed you see is the real speed, and nothing was edited or sped up. The characters are made by AI models, so the odd shapes are part of the fun.
>
> You can try it in your browser and pick a character: https://conquercourse.pages.dev/?ref=linkedin-launch
>
