# Film craft for the hook and the 3 minute film (CC-150)

*Serves the 20 s hook and the demo film in [storyboard](storyboard.md) (CC-143).*
*Written after the product owner's review of the first hook cut; see the mistakes list below.*

## So what
- Five rules would have prevented every problem the product owner named:
  - A close-up must show one readable thing that matters (the foot shot broke this).
  - A camera move needs a cause on screen, and a cause is a moving subject (the flying hand and gameplay cameras broke this).
  - One idea per shot, and the shot is long enough to read (a 1.8 s shot with a move and a label did not give the eye time).
  - Keep screen direction and lead room, so the runner always has space to run into (the orbits broke this).
  - Cut on the action, from one angle to a different angle of the same action, so the cut hides itself.
- What worked (the fly-over and the slow-motion drop) already follows these rules: a clear subject, a cause for the move, and room to read.
- Evidence level: most sources are tutorial blogs and one study; none is an academic rule book, and sports and parkour camera language is thin (see "What we could not verify").

## Tips, with sources
- Open on the most striking gameplay and show the genre by movement.
  - Source: https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-trailer-best-practices (quotes Valve: the first trailer should be "primarily gameplay, showing the player what they will be doing").
  - For ConquerCourse this means the runner in a real course appears in the first 2 to 3 s, and the park photo is only a setup, not the hook.
- The first 5 to 10 s must work with the sound off, because Steam autoplays muted.
  - Source: https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-trailer-best-practices
  - For ConquerCourse this means we watch every cut on mute and ask whether the story still reads.
- Shots of about 2 to 4 s, with varied rhythm; all rapid cuts exhaust and all slow pans bore.
  - Source: https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-trailer-best-practices
  - For ConquerCourse this means 1.8 s is the bottom of the range, so a 1.8 s shot may hold only one simple thing and one label.
- Show the real play camera and perspective by seconds 5 to 15.
  - Source: https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-trailer-best-practices
  - For ConquerCourse this means one side-on view of the runner is the "home" shot, and we return to it so the viewer learns it.
- Do not bury the hook, and do not cram: one focus beats many.
  - Source: https://game-wisdom.com/?p=33294 and https://bugnet.io/blog/common-game-trailer-mistakes (search summaries, not read in full).
  - For ConquerCourse this means the hook sells one idea: a real park becomes a course, and the let-go carries on.
- Busy, dark, choppy footage is hard to follow; start with a simple uncluttered shot, then speed up.
  - Source: https://thenextweb.com/gaming/2018/08/02/theres-a-reason-why-video-game-trailers-are-so-confusing/ (search summary).
  - For ConquerCourse this means every capture needs a clean background and a runner that contrasts with it.
- Close-ups are read faster than wide shots, because a big subject with little clutter is quick to read.
  - Source: https://as.cornell.edu/news/heres-looking-you-kid-filmmakers-know-how-we-read-emotions (about 31,000 shots in 24 films; the speed test used faces).
  - For ConquerCourse this means a close-up can be short only if it is uncluttered and shows something already known, and a wide fly-over needs 3 s or more.
- The study tested faces, so "a close-up reads fast" is proven for faces and only assumed for hands and feet.
  - Source: same Cornell page.
  - For ConquerCourse this means a foot or hand close-up needs context first (we saw the runner) and a visible event inside it (contact, grip, release).
- A close-up is a unit of the same size as a head filling the frame; an extreme close-up of a small part is a special tool.
  - Source: https://shea.mit.edu/ramparts/commentaryguides/glossary/filmlexicon.htm
  - For ConquerCourse this means a foot filling the frame is an extreme close-up, and it needs a wider shot of the same action just before it.
- Establish first, then go closer.
  - Source: https://shea.mit.edu/ramparts/commentaryguides/glossary/filmlexicon.htm (establishing shot: a complete view of the setting that orients the viewer before closer shots).
  - For ConquerCourse this means the fly-over comes before any macro of the course, not after.
- Camera movement should be motivated by on-screen action, a change in what the viewer needs to know, or a feeling.
  - Source: https://nofilmschool.com/2018/06/key-good-camera-movement-motivation ("Good camera movement doesn't have to be fancy, it just has to be motivated").
  - Also: https://howtofilmschool.com/dictionary/motivated-camera-movement/ (search summary).
  - For ConquerCourse this means the camera only moves because the runner moves or because we are revealing the course.
- A moving camera with a moving subject adds energy; a locked camera on a calm subject reads as calm.
  - Source: https://nofilmschool.com/2018/06/key-good-camera-movement-motivation
  - For ConquerCourse this means the swing is shot from a locked or constant-speed side camera, and the orbit is kept for the end card or the character reveal.
- Moves are tracking (steady, one direction) or pans (pivot); a fast blur pan is a whip pan.
  - Source: https://shea.mit.edu/ramparts/commentaryguides/glossary/filmlexicon.htm
  - For ConquerCourse this means we name each move in the shot list, and an "orbit around a swinging runner" is two moves fighting each other.
- Give a moving subject lead room, the space ahead of it.
  - Source: https://wolfcrow.com/the-three-important-composition-and-framing-conventions-in-cinematography/ (more room on the side the subject moves toward than behind).
  - For ConquerCourse this means the runner sits about a third in from the edge they came from, with the open two thirds ahead of them.
- Headroom: a little for wide shots, less for close-ups; sources disagree on the exact amount.
  - Sources: https://wolfcrow.com/the-three-important-composition-and-framing-conventions-in-cinematography/ and https://mimolive.com/framing-and-composition/ (search summary).
  - For ConquerCourse this means leave clear sky above a jumper so the let-go arc is not cut off.
- The rule of thirds is a guide, not a law; off-centre subjects often look better.
  - Source: https://wolfcrow.com/the-three-important-composition-and-framing-conventions-in-cinematography/
  - For ConquerCourse this means the runner and the bar sit on a third line, not the middle.
- 180-degree rule: stay on one side of the line of action, and a subject that leaves frame left enters the next shot from the right.
  - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/180-degree_rule and https://shea.mit.edu/ramparts/commentaryguides/glossary/filmlexicon.htm
  - For ConquerCourse this means the runner goes left to right in every shot, and the line is the course lane.
- 30-degree rule: a new shot of the same thing must differ in angle or size enough to avoid a jump cut.
  - Source: https://shea.mit.edu/ramparts/commentaryguides/glossary/filmlexicon.htm
  - For ConquerCourse this means two shots of the bar in a row must change both size and angle, or be one shot.
- Cut on the action, in the middle of a movement, so the viewer watches the move and not the edit.
  - Sources: https://shea.mit.edu/ramparts/commentaryguides/glossary/filmlexicon.htm and the match-on-action definition in https://cursa.app/en/exercises/video-edition/continuity-editing-techniques-for-clear-visual-storytelling (search summary).
  - For ConquerCourse this means we cut in the middle of the jump to the catch, and in the middle of the let-go to the slow motion.
- Cut on the beat of the music.
  - Source: https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-trailer-best-practices
  - For ConquerCourse this means we keep whole-beat cuts at 100 bpm, as already measured in [storyboard](storyboard.md).
- Speed ramping changes playback speed over time, common in sports highlights; a ramp, not a switch, keeps the eye.
  - Source: https://motionarray.com/premiere-pro/premiere-pro-speed-ramping-tutorial/ (search summary, about 50 fps or more of source is needed to avoid judder).
  - For ConquerCourse this means our stepped clock already draws every frame, so the 0.25 times speed is smooth, and we ramp into it over 2 to 3 frames instead of snapping.
- Text goes on top of gameplay, not on separate cards, and a half-text trailer feels like a slideshow.
  - Source: https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-trailer-best-practices
  - For ConquerCourse this means one short label per shot at most, on the footage, never over the runner.
- Broadcast habit: a wide camera for the whole picture and a closer camera for key moments; nothing precise was found for ninja-warrior shows.
  - Sources: a two-camera sports guide (low trust) and https://www.dga.org/craft/dgaq/issues/1903-summer-2019/american-ninja-warrior (director plans every camera position; not read in full).
  - For ConquerCourse this means "wide for the course, medium for the move, close only for the key moment" is our house rule.

## Checklist for every shot
- Is the subject readable at thumbnail size (a phone-sized frame)?
- Can a stranger say what it is in under a second?
- One idea per shot; is there a second idea or a second label?
- Is the camera still, or is its move caused by the subject or a reveal?
- Is the move smooth, at constant speed, and in one direction?
- Does the moving subject have room ahead of it and sky above it?
- Does the runner go left to right, like the shots before and after?
- Is this a close-up, and if so did a wider shot of the same thing come first?
- Does the shot last long enough to read: about 2 s for simple, 3 s or more for wide or busy?
- Does the cut in and out land on a beat and in the middle of an action?
- Is the next shot different in size or angle (30-degree rule)?
- Does it still make sense with the sound off?
- Is there clutter (labels, particles, bright background) fighting the subject?
- Is it honest: tagged REAL, META or GFX as in [film-toolchain](film-toolchain.md)?

## Mistakes in our first hook, mapped to rules
- Foot close-up at 3 s, "far too zoomed and meaningless" (shot 3 in [storyboard](storyboard.md)):
  - Broke: close-ups need context and an event; establish before going close; one readable thing.
  - Why: nothing showed whose foot it was, and a boot on grass has no visible change inside the frame.
- Hand-on-bar shot with a camera "flying everywhere" (shot 5, small orbit):
  - Broke: motivated movement; two moves at once (an orbit on a swinging subject).
  - Why: the orbit had no cause, and the hand moved in the frame as well as the camera.
- Real gameplay capture with a camera "flying everywhere" (shots 6 to 8):
  - Broke: motivated movement, lead room, and screen direction.
  - Why: a camera that tracks a fast swing with lag overshoots, and the orbit on the landing flips the side we view from.
- Each of shots 2 to 8 is 1.8 s with a move and a label:
  - Broke: one idea per shot, and time to read.
  - Why: the viewer reads the label or the move, not both.
- The storyboard rule "every shot has a move" came from a hardware film with macro product shots:
  - Broke: copying a reference rule that does not suit a moving subject.
  - Fix: the move belongs to the subject first, and the camera adds a move only for the fly-over and one gentle push.
- Whip cuts at 2 to 3, 4 to 5 and 8 to 9:
  - Broke: cut on the action; a whip hides the cut but also hides what to look at.
  - Fix: keep one whip at most, into the fly-over.

- The two shots the owner liked (the fly-over, the slow-motion drop) stay, near their old times.

| 14.4 to 16.2 | the landing | same side, runner going right | locked, then a small follow | the speed has to go somewhere |

- Changes from the first cut:
  - The foot macro is gone; the runner shot at 4.2 s says "a runner" instead.
  - The only close-up is the catch, and it comes after a wide shot of the same action.
  - The hand and gameplay shots have no orbit; the camera is locked or moves at a constant speed with the runner.
  - Label text: at most one label per shot, and shots 3, 5 and 6 carry none.
- Open points for the owner:
  - The end card is 1.8 s, down from 2.4 s; a 33-beat total leaves no more room, so one earlier shot could give up a beat.
  - A 9:16 version needs the runner and the open space ahead of them both inside a narrow frame.

## What we could not verify
- No source gave a measured minimum time a viewer needs for a wide shot; the 2 to 4 s range is trailer-guide advice, not a measurement.
- The Cornell study tested faces; hands, feet and bars are our assumption.
- Sports, parkour and ninja-warrior camera language: the searches returned only general multi-camera sports guides and a DGA profile we did not read in full, so the house rule above is our reading.
- Several tips (marked "search summary") come from pages we saw only as search results, not read in full.
- The 3-second "adjust to a shot change" claim turned up in a slideshow and could not be traced, so it is not used.
- We did not view the reference video or our hook for this note; the mistakes list uses the product owner's words and the shot table in [storyboard](storyboard.md).
- Remotion and ffmpeg practicalities were out of scope here; see [film-toolchain](film-toolchain.md).
